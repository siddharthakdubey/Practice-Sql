Window ranking

ROW_NUMBER, RANK, DENSE_RANK, NTILE, PERCENT_RANK and CUME_DIST: put rows in order, split them in buckets, pick the top N per group, remove duplicates.

After this topic you will choose the right ranking function when values tie, build top-N-per-group and "latest row per key" queries, remove duplicates, and split data into equal buckets.

Ranking functions need ORDER BY, and take no arguments

SELECT OrderID, Sales,
       ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY Sales DESC, OrderID) AS RowNum,
       RANK()       OVER (ORDER BY Sales DESC)          AS Rnk,
       DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY Sales DESC)          AS DenseRnk
FROM Orders;

PARTITION BY: rank inside each group

SELECT FirstName, Department, Salary,
       RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY Department ORDER BY Salary DESC) AS DeptRank
FROM Employees;
FirstName | Department | Salary | DeptRank Kevin | Marketing | 65000 | 1 Frank | Marketing | 55000 | 2 Michael | Sales | 90000 | 1 Mary | Sales | 75000 | 2 Carol | Sales | 55000 | 3

Top-N per group

You cannot filter on a window function in WHERE, so rank in a CTE/subquery, then filter:

WITH r AS (
    SELECT OrderID, CustomerID, Sales,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY CustomerID ORDER BY Sales DESC, OrderID) AS rn
    FROM Orders
)
SELECT OrderID, CustomerID, Sales FROM r WHERE rn = 1;      -- the biggest order of each customer
OrderID | CustomerID | Sales 1008 | 1 | 50 1006 | 2 | 25 1002 | 3 | 60 1010 | 4 | 45 1004 | 6 | 90

Use rn <= 3 for "top 3 per customer". Choose RANK instead of ROW_NUMBER if you want all tied winners.

SQL Server shortcutSELECT TOP 1 WITH TIES * FROM Orders ORDER BY Sales DESC does a global top-1 including ties, no window needed. The window approach is what you need for "per group".

Latest row per key, and removing duplicates

-- latest order of every customer
SELECT * FROM (
   SELECT *, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY CustomerID ORDER BY OrderDate DESC) AS rn FROM Orders
) t WHERE rn = 1;

-- delete duplicates, keeping one row per (CustomerID, ProductID) -- a very common interview task
WITH d AS (
   SELECT ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY CustomerID, ProductID ORDER BY OrderID) AS rn FROM Orders
)
DELETE FROM d WHERE rn > 1;           -- SQL Server lets you delete through a CTE

The sample Orders table has no such duplicates, so the DELETE would remove nothing. To see it work, insert a copy of an order first (new OrderID, same customer and product), and run the whole experiment inside BEGIN TRAN ... ROLLBACK.

NTILE(n): equal buckets

SELECT OrderID, Sales, NTILE(3) OVER (ORDER BY Sales DESC, OrderID) AS Bucket FROM Orders;
OrderID | Sales | Bucket 1004 | 90 | 1 1002 | 60 | 1 1008 | 50 | 1 1010 | 45 | 1 1005 | 40 | 2 1007 | 40 | 2 1006 | 25 | 2 1001 | 20 | 3 1009 | 20 | 3 1003 | 10 | 3

When rows do not divide evenly, the first buckets get one extra row. Uses: quartiles/deciles, "top 20% customers" (NTILE(5) bucket 1), splitting work into equal batches. Rows with equal values may land in different buckets (here the two 40s are together by luck, but they could be split).

PERCENT_RANK and CUME_DIST: relative position

SELECT OrderID, Sales,
       PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY Sales) AS PctRank,   -- (rank - 1) / (rows - 1), from 0 to 1
       CUME_DIST()    OVER (ORDER BY Sales) AS CumeDist   -- share of rows with value <= this one
FROM Orders;
OrderID | Sales | PctRank | CumeDist 1003 | 10 | 0 | 0.1 1001 | 20 | 0.111 | 0.3 1009 | 20 | 0.111 | 0.3 1006 | 25 | 0.333 | 0.4 1005 | 40 | 0.444 | 0.6 1007 | 40 | 0.444 | 0.6 1010 | 45 | 0.667 | 0.7 1008 | 50 | 0.778 | 0.8 1002 | 60 | 0.889 | 0.9 1004 | 90 | 1 | 1

"This order is bigger than 77.8% of the others" (PERCENT_RANK), "70% of orders are at most this big" (CUME_DIST). Handy for percentile thresholds.

Remember

  • Always add a tie-breaker to ROW_NUMBER's ORDER BY (a unique column) for repeatable results.
  • NULLs sort first in ASC, last in DESC; they get ranked like any value. Filter or push them with CASE if needed.
  • Ranking ignores frames (ROWS/RANGE).
  • Performance: an index matching (PARTITION BY cols, ORDER BY cols) lets SQL Server skip the sort step (topic 30, 31).
