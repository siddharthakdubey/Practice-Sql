Aggregate functions

Turn many rows into summary rows: COUNT, SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX and the GROUP BY that decides the groups.

After this topic you will summarise data with every common aggregate, group by one or several columns, filter groups with HAVING, understand how NULL and DISTINCT affect results, and spot the join "fan-out" error.

The five core aggregates

FunctionReturnsNULLs
COUNT(*)number of rowscounted
COUNT(col)rows where col is not NULLignored
COUNT(DISTINCT col)number of different non-NULL valuesignored
SUM(col)total (numbers only)ignored
AVG(col)sum / count of non-NULLignored
MIN(col) / MAX(col)smallest / largest (numbers, text, dates)ignored
SELECT COUNT(*)                    AS Orders,          -- 10
       COUNT(ShipDate)             AS Shipped,         -- 9  (one NULL)
       COUNT(DISTINCT CustomerID)  AS Buyers,          -- 5  (customers 1,2,3,4 and unknown 6)
       SUM(Sales)                  AS Revenue,         -- 400
       AVG(Sales)                  AS AvgOrder,        -- 40
       MIN(OrderDate)              AS FirstOrder,      -- 2025-01-01
       MAX(Sales)                  AS BiggestOrder     -- 90
FROM Orders;
AVG of integersOn an INT column AVG returns an INT (decimals dropped). The ten quantities add up to 19, so AVG(Quantity) returns 1, while AVG(Quantity * 1.0) returns 1.9.

GROUP BY: one result row per group

SELECT CustomerID, COUNT(*) AS Orders, SUM(Sales) AS Total
FROM   Orders
GROUP BY CustomerID;
RuleMeaning
Everything in SELECT is either grouped or aggregatedSELECT CustomerID, OrderStatus, SUM(Sales) ... GROUP BY CustomerID fails: OrderStatus is neither.
You may group by something not in SELECTallowed, but the output is hard to read
Aggregates can't go in WHEREuse HAVING
All NULLs form one group

Group by several columns

SELECT CustomerID, OrderStatus, COUNT(*) AS Orders, SUM(Sales) AS Total
FROM Orders
GROUP BY CustomerID, OrderStatus
ORDER BY CustomerID, OrderStatus;
CustomerID | OrderStatus | Orders | Total 1 | Delivered | 2 | 60 2 | Delivered | 2 | 45 2 | Shipped | 1 | 20 3 | Processing | 1 | 40 3 | Shipped | 1 | 60 4 | Delivered | 2 | 85 6 | Shipped | 1 | 90

HAVING: filter the groups

SELECT CustomerID, SUM(Sales) AS Total
FROM Orders
WHERE OrderStatus <> 'Processing'      -- rows first
GROUP BY CustomerID
HAVING SUM(Sales) > 70                   -- then groups
ORDER BY Total DESC;
CustomerID | Total 6 | 90 4 | 85

Customer 3's 60 (Shipped only, Processing removed by WHERE) and customer 2's 65 and customer 1's 60 are all below 70. Put row filters in WHERE (cheaper: fewer rows to group), group filters in HAVING.

More aggregates

SELECT Country,
       STRING_AGG(FirstName, ', ') AS People,       -- join text from rows into one string
       STDEV(Score) AS StdDev, VAR(Score) AS Variance   -- statistics
FROM Customers GROUP BY Country;

SELECT COUNT_BIG(*), CHECKSUM_AGG(CHECKSUM(*)) FROM Orders;     -- bigger counts, quick change detection

Subtotals and grand totals: ROLLUP, CUBE, GROUPING SETS

SELECT Country, SUM(Score) AS TotalScore
FROM Customers
GROUP BY ROLLUP (Country);
Country | TotalScore Germany | 850 USA | 1650 NULL | 2500

ROLLUP(a, b) adds subtotals for a then a grand total. CUBE(a, b) gives all combinations. GROUPING SETS ((a), (b), ()) lets you choose. GROUPING(col) returns 1 on the rows that are subtotals, so you can label them: CASE WHEN GROUPING(Country)=1 THEN 'ALL' ELSE Country END.

The fan-out trap (wrong totals after a join)

-- WRONG: each customer's Score is repeated once per order
SELECT SUM(c.Score) FROM Customers c JOIN Orders o ON o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID;
-- Kevin's 900 is added 3 times, Mary's 750 twice ...: 5,900 instead of the true 2,500 (customers 1 to 4)
RuleBefore summing a column, ask: "is this column unique per row after my join?" If not, aggregate each table separately first (CTE or subquery) and then join the summaries. Always compare the row count before and after a join.

Order of work

FROM/JOIN → WHERE → GROUP BY → aggregates → HAVING → SELECT → ORDER BY. Aggregates are computed inside the group step. Window functions (next topics) come later, after GROUP BY.
