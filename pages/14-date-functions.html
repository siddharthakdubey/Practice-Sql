Date and time functions

Get "now", pull out parts, add and subtract time, count differences, format, and filter by period correctly.

After this topic you will use GETDATE, YEAR/MONTH/DAY, DATEPART, DATENAME, EOMONTH, DATEADD, DATEDIFF, FORMAT/CONVERT, and write date filters that are both correct and fast.

Date types

TypeHoldsExample
DATEdate only2025-03-15
TIMEtime only14:30:00.123
DATETIME2(n)date + time, precision n (0-7)2025-03-15 14:30:00.1234567
DATETIMEOFFSETdate + time + time-zone offset2025-03-15 14:30 +05:30
DATETIME (old)3 ms precision, limited rangeavoid in new designs
Banking habitStore timestamps in UTC (SYSUTCDATETIME()) and convert at the edges, exactly as this portal project does.

Now

SELECT GETDATE()            -- current server date+time (DATETIME)
     , SYSDATETIME()        -- more precise (DATETIME2)
     , SYSUTCDATETIME()     -- in UTC
     , CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE) AS Today;   -- drop the time part

Extract parts

SELECT OrderID, OrderDate,
       YEAR(OrderDate)                    AS Yr,        -- 2025
       MONTH(OrderDate)                   AS Mth,       -- 1..12
       DAY(OrderDate)                     AS Dy,
       DATEPART(QUARTER, OrderDate)       AS Qtr,       -- 1..4
       DATEPART(WEEK, OrderDate)          AS WkNo,
       DATEPART(WEEKDAY, OrderDate)       AS DayNo,     -- depends on SET DATEFIRST (US default: 1 = Sunday)
       DATENAME(MONTH, OrderDate)         AS MonthName, -- 'January'
       DATENAME(WEEKDAY, OrderDate)       AS DayName    -- 'Wednesday'
FROM Orders WHERE OrderID IN (1001, 1005);
OrderID | OrderDate | Yr | Mth | Dy | Qtr | WkNo | DayNo | MonthName | DayName 1001 | 2025-01-01 | 2025 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 4 | January | Wednesday 1005 | 2025-02-01 | 2025 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 5 | 7 | February | Saturday

DATEPART returns a number, DATENAME returns text. The part names (YEAR, QUARTER, MONTH, DAYOFYEAR, DAY, WEEK, WEEKDAY, HOUR...) are keywords, not strings: no quotes.

Group by a period

SELECT YEAR(OrderDate) AS Yr, MONTH(OrderDate) AS Mth, COUNT(*) AS Orders, SUM(Sales) AS Total
FROM Orders
GROUP BY YEAR(OrderDate), MONTH(OrderDate)
ORDER BY Yr, Mth;
Yr | Mth | Orders | Total 2025 | 1 | 4 | 180 2025 | 2 | 3 | 105 2025 | 3 | 3 | 115

First/last day of a period

SELECT EOMONTH('2025-02-10')                                   -- 2025-02-28  (end of month; handles leap years)
     , EOMONTH('2025-02-10', 1)                                -- 2025-03-31  (offset in months)
     , DATEFROMPARTS(2025, 2, 1)                               -- 2025-02-01  build a date
     , DATETRUNC(MONTH, CAST('2025-02-10' AS DATE))            -- 2025-02-01  (SQL Server 2022+)
     , DATEADD(DAY, 1, EOMONTH('2025-02-10', -1))              -- 2025-02-01  first day of month, works everywhere
;

DATEADD: move a date

SELECT DATEADD(DAY,   10, '2025-01-25')   -- 2025-02-04
     , DATEADD(MONTH,  1, '2025-01-31')   -- 2025-02-28   (clamps to the last valid day)
     , DATEADD(YEAR,  -1, '2025-03-15')   -- 2024-03-15   (negative = go back)
     , DATEADD(HOUR,   5, '2025-01-01 09:00');
-- orders expected to arrive 7 days after shipping
SELECT OrderID, ShipDate, DATEADD(DAY, 7, ShipDate) AS Expected FROM Orders;

DATEDIFF: count the gap

SELECT OrderID, OrderDate, ShipDate,
       DATEDIFF(DAY, OrderDate, ShipDate) AS DaysToShip
FROM Orders;
OrderID | OrderDate | ShipDate | DaysToShip 1001 | 2025-01-01 | 2025-01-05 | 4 1002 | 2025-01-05 | 2025-01-10 | 5 1003 | 2025-01-10 | 2025-01-25 | 15 1007 | 2025-02-15 | NULL | NULL

Syntax: DATEDIFF(part, start, end), answer = end - start in whole parts. NULL ship date gives NULL.

DATEDIFF counts boundaries crossed, not elapsed time DATEDIFF(YEAR, '2024-12-31', '2025-01-01') = 1 although only one day passed, because the year boundary was crossed once. For an exact age use extra logic (compare month/day) or DATEDIFF(DAY...)/365.25. Watch this:

Formatting and converting to text

SELECT FORMAT(OrderDate, 'dd-MMM-yyyy')   -- 01-Jan-2025   (.NET format strings, flexible but slow on big tables)
     , FORMAT(OrderDate, 'yyyy-MM')       -- 2025-01
     , CONVERT(VARCHAR(10), OrderDate, 23)  -- 2025-01-01   (style 23 = ISO yyyy-mm-dd)
     , CONVERT(VARCHAR(10), OrderDate, 103) -- 01/01/2025   (British dd/mm/yyyy, the India style)
     , CONVERT(VARCHAR(10), OrderDate, 101) -- 01/01/2025   (US mm/dd/yyyy)
FROM Orders WHERE OrderID = 1001;
AmbiguityIs '03/04/2025' 3 April or 4 March? It depends on language settings. Always write dates as 'YYYY-MM-DD' in code (use 'YYYYMMDD' to be safe with DATETIME) and keep dates as DATE columns, not strings.
SELECT TRY_CAST('2025-02-30' AS DATE);   -- NULL: February 30 is not real, no crash
SELECT ISDATE('2025-02-28');             -- 1

Filtering by period (correct and fast)

-- All of February 2025. Half-open interval: >= start AND < next start
SELECT * FROM Orders
WHERE OrderDate >= '2025-02-01' AND OrderDate < '2025-03-01';

-- Slow and index-blind (function on the column):
-- WHERE YEAR(OrderDate) = 2025 AND MONTH(OrderDate) = 2

-- Last 30 days up to now
WHERE OrderDate >= DATEADD(DAY, -30, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
Half-open intervals"On or after the first day, strictly before the next period." They work for DATE, DATETIME and DATETIME2 with no midnight/23:59:59.997 tricks, and they let the optimizer use an index.

Time zones (India relevance)

SELECT SYSDATETIMEOFFSET();                                              -- local with offset, e.g. +05:30
SELECT SYSUTCDATETIME() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC' AT TIME ZONE 'India Standard Time';   -- UTC converted to IST
